Search Degradation Postmortem

Summary
On 2 June 2026 product search returned errors or empty results for about two hours, from 09:15 to 11:20 UTC. Browsing by category and checkout kept working, but customers who relied on the search box could not find products. The degradation was caused by a full disk on two of the six search cluster nodes, which made the cluster reject new writes and then fail queries against the affected shards.

Timeline
At 09:15 UTC the nightly re-indexing job, which had been delayed by a slow upstream export, was still running when morning traffic started. Its temporary files filled the data disk on two nodes. The disk usage alert fired at 09:17 and paged the on-call engineer for the search team, who acknowledged it within four minutes.

The engineer first suspected a network problem, because the errors looked like connection timeouts between the search service and the cluster. It took until 10:05 to see that the two nodes had switched their indices to read-only mode to protect themselves. Deleting the temporary files and clearing the read-only flag restored writes at 10:40, and all shards were healthy again at 11:20.

Root Cause
The re-indexing job wrote its temporary files to the same disk as the live indices, and nothing limited how much space it could use. On a normal night it finishes before 06:00 and cleans up after itself, so the problem only appears when the job overruns into daytime traffic. The cluster's protective read-only mode worked as designed, but the search service turned the resulting errors into empty result pages instead of an error message, which delayed diagnosis.

Impact
Around 41,000 searches failed or returned no results. Conversion from search sessions dropped by roughly 60 percent during the incident, and the estimated revenue impact is €95,000. No data was lost and no customer was charged incorrectly.

What Went Well
The alert reached the right person immediately, and the runbook for read-only indices was accurate once the right cause was identified.
