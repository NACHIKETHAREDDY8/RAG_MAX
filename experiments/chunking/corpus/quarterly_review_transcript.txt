Okay, let's get started, thanks everyone for joining the Q3 business review. I'll begin with the numbers. Revenue for the third quarter came in at 4.2 million euros, which is up 12 percent compared with the same quarter last year. Most of that growth came from enterprise renewals, where we renewed 31 of our 33 large accounts. The small business segment was the weak spot, it actually shrank by 3 percent. Our read is that the price increase we introduced in July pushed some of the smaller customers to cheaper competitors. Finance wants to revisit the small business pricing before the end of the year, so expect a proposal in November. Gross margin held steady at 71 percent. Next item is hiring. As of this week there is a freeze on all non-engineering roles until February. Engineering is the exception, we still plan to hire six backend engineers this quarter because the platform roadmap depends on them. We also cancelled the contract with the TalentBridge recruiting agency, which saves about 80 thousand euros a year, and recruiting will be handled in-house by Dana's team from now on. If you have open requisitions outside engineering, please pause them in the applicant tracking system today. Moving on to the data center migration. The plan is still to leave the Frankfurt colocation facility and run everything in our cloud region by the end of November. So far two of the five core services have been migrated, search and notifications. The billing service is blocked. The problem is licensing, our Oracle database license does not allow us to run it on the cloud provider's hardware without buying a much more expensive agreement. The workaround the platform team proposed is to move billing to PostgreSQL, and they estimate that will take six weeks, which is tight but still fits the November deadline. The remaining two services, reporting and the customer portal, have no known blockers. Next, customer churn in the Asia Pacific region. APAC churn went up to 9 percent this quarter, from 5 percent last quarter, which is well above every other region. When we interviewed the customers who left, the main reason they gave was the lack of support in Japanese, several of them said tickets took days to resolve because of translation. The plan is to hire two Japanese-speaking support agents in Tokyo and to localise the onboarding flow into Japanese by the end of Q1. Customer success will also call every APAC account above 50 thousand euros in annual value before the end of October. Now the security audit. The external audit by Castell Security finished last week, and they reported three high-severity findings. The most serious one is that API keys used by the analytics pipeline have never been rotated, some of them are more than three years old. The decision is that all of those keys will be rotated by the 15th of October, and from then on keys will rotate automatically every 90 days. The other two findings are about overly broad admin permissions in the support tool and missing audit logs for data exports, and both have owners and should be fixed this quarter. Last topic, a couple of announcements. The new Lisbon office opens in January with 20 desks, mostly for the customer success team. And the company offsite is moving from Lisbon to Porto, it will now be on the 12th to the 14th of March. That's everything for today, thanks all.
